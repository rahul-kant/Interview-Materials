# Mock Interview Strategy & Plan

## Overview
Mock interviews are **critical** for senior positions. They simulate real interview pressure, identify weak areas, and build confidence. This guide provides a structured approach to mock interviews.

---

## Why Mock Interviews Matter

### For Senior Positions (14 YOE):
- ✅ **Build confidence** in communicating complex ideas
- ✅ **Identify blind spots** you might not notice alone
- ✅ **Practice timing** - crucial in 45-min rounds
- ✅ **Get feedback** from experienced interviewers
- ✅ **Reduce anxiety** through familiarity
- ✅ **Refine stories** for behavioral rounds
- ✅ **Practice thinking out loud** - key skill

**Statistics**: Candidates who do 5+ mocks have **2-3x higher** pass rates.

---

## Mock Interview Schedule

### Phase 1: Early Practice (Weeks 1-8)
**Goal**: Get comfortable, identify major gaps

**Frequency**: 1 mock every 2 weeks
**Focus**: Technical fundamentals
**Partners**: Peers, friends, online platforms

**Breakdown:**
- Week 2: 1 Coding mock (Easy-Medium problems)
- Week 4: 1 Coding mock (Medium problems)
- Week 6: 1 System Design mock (basic)
- Week 8: 1 Behavioral mock

**Total**: 4 mocks

---

### Phase 2: Intensive Practice (Weeks 9-12)
**Goal**: Build consistency, improve weak areas

**Frequency**: 2 mocks per week
**Focus**: Full interview simulation
**Partners**: Mix of peers and paid professionals

**Breakdown:**
- Week 9: 1 Coding + 1 System Design
- Week 10: 1 Coding + 1 Behavioral
- Week 11: 1 System Design + 1 Behavioral
- Week 12: 2 Full rounds (Coding + System Design)

**Total**: 8 mocks

---

### Phase 3: Final Polish (Weeks 13-16)
**Goal**: Company-specific preparation, peak performance

**Frequency**: 3-4 mocks per week
**Focus**: Company-specific, full interview days
**Partners**: FAANG interviewers (paid platforms)

**Breakdown:**
- Week 13: 2 Coding + 1 System Design + 1 Behavioral
- Week 14: 1 Full mock day (4-5 rounds)
- Week 15: Company-specific mocks (2-3)
- Week 16: 1-2 final tune-up mocks

**Total**: 10-12 mocks

**Grand Total**: 22-24 mock interviews over 4 months

## Types of Mock Interviews

### 1. Coding Mocks

**Duration**: 45-60 minutes
**Structure**:
- 5 mins: Introduction
- 35-40 mins: Problem solving (1-2 problems)
- 10-15 mins: Questions & feedback

**What to Practice:**
- Problem understanding & clarification
- Thinking out loud
- Multiple approaches (brute force → optimal)
- Clean code with good naming
- Testing with examples
- Time/space complexity analysis
- Edge case handling

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Problem-solving approach
- Code quality
- Communication
- Optimization
- Testing
- Time management

---

### 2. System Design Mocks

**Duration**: 45-60 minutes
**Structure**:
- 5 mins: Requirements clarification
- 5 mins: Back-of-envelope calculations
- 10 mins: High-level design
- 20 mins: Deep dive
- 5 mins: Trade-offs discussion
- 5 mins: Questions & feedback

**What to Practice:**
- Driving the conversation (you lead!)
- Requirements gathering
- Capacity estimation
- Component design
- Trade-off analysis
- Scalability discussion
- Handling follow-up questions

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Structured approach
- Scalability thinking
- Trade-off analysis
- Communication clarity
- Depth of knowledge
- Practical experience

---

### 3. Behavioral Mocks

**Duration**: 45-60 minutes
**Structure**:
- 5 mins: Introduction
- 35-40 mins: 4-6 behavioral questions
- 10 mins: Questions & feedback

**What to Practice:**
- STAR method execution
- Story delivery (5-8 mins each)
- Showing impact with metrics
- Handling follow-up probes
- Natural delivery (not memorized)
- Authentic responses

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Story structure (STAR)
- Specificity & metrics
- Leadership demonstration
- Self-awareness
- Learning from experiences
- Natural communication

---

### 4. Full Mock Day

**Duration**: 4-5 hours
**Structure**:
- Round 1: Coding (45 mins)
- Break (10 mins)
- Round 2: Coding (45 mins)
- Break (10 mins)
- Round 3: System Design (60 mins)
- Break (10 mins)
- Round 4: Behavioral (45 mins)
- Feedback session (30 mins)

**What to Practice:**
- Stamina (mental endurance)
- Consistent performance
- Staying focused across rounds
- Context switching
- Managing stress
- Bouncing back from mistakes

## Conducting Effective Mocks

### As Interviewee

**Before the Mock:**
1. Choose a problem/topic you haven't seen
2. Set up environment (whiteboard/IDE)
3. Have notebook ready
4. Treat it seriously (dress code, quiet space)
5. Turn off distractions

**During the Mock:**
1. Introduce yourself briefly
2. Ask clarifying questions
3. Think out loud constantly
4. Write clean code/draw clear diagrams
5. Test your solution
6. Manage time actively
7. Ask questions at the end

**After the Mock:**
1. Take notes on feedback
2. Self-reflect (what went well/poorly)
3. Identify specific areas to improve
4. Practice those areas before next mock
5. Don't be discouraged by poor performance

---

### As Interviewer (For Peers)

**Preparation:**
1. Choose a good problem (Medium difficulty)
2. Know the optimal solution
3. Prepare follow-up questions
4. Have rubric ready

**During Interview:**
1. Be friendly, reduce stress
2. Give hints if stuck (don't let flounder)
3. Note communication, approach, code quality
4. Time management
5. Ask follow-ups on solution

**Feedback:**
1. Start with positives
2. Specific, actionable feedback
3. Compare to expected level
4. Suggest improvement areas
5. Encourage, don't discourage

## Common Mistakes in Mocks

### ❌ Don't:

1. **Take it lightly**: "It's just a mock"
   - Treat it like real interview

2. **Skip feedback session**: Rush off after
   - Feedback is the most valuable part

3. **Get defensive**: Argue with feedback
   - Listen, learn, improve

4. **Do the same problems**: Repeat easy wins
   - Challenge yourself with new problems

5. **Only do one type**: All coding, no system design
   - Balanced practice across all types

6. **Wait too long**: First mock in week 15
   - Start early, iterate often

7. **Ignore weak areas**: Keep doing what you're good at
   - Focus on weaknesses

8. **No follow-up**: Don't practice feedback points
   - Actively work on improvement areas
