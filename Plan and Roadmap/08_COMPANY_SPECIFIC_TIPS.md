# Company-Specific Interview Tips

## Overview
Each FAANG company has unique interview styles, culture, and focus areas. This guide provides specific tips for Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, and Netflix.

---

## 1. Amazon

### Company Culture
- **Leadership Principles (LPs)**: 16 principles drive everything
- **Customer Obsession**: Start with customer, work backwards
- **Ownership Mentality**: Think long-term, own outcomes
- **Frugality**: Do more with less
- **High Standards**: Raise the bar continuously

### Interview Process

**Timeline**: 4-6 weeks

**Rounds:**
1. **Phone Screen** (1 hour)
   - 1-2 coding problems
   - Brief LP questions

2. **Onsite/Virtual** (4-5 hours)
   - **Loop 1-2**: Coding (2 rounds, 45 mins each)
   - **Loop 3**: System Design (1 hour)
   - **Loop 4-5**: Behavioral/LP focus (2 rounds)
   - **Bar Raiser**: Special round with senior interviewer

### Coding Interview
**Focus Areas:**
- Arrays, Strings, Hash Tables
- Trees, Graphs
- Dynamic Programming
- OOP Design

**Common Questions:**
- Two Sum variations
- LRU Cache
- Merge K Sorted Lists
- Number of Islands
- Word Ladder
- Design Underground System

**Difficulty:** Medium to Hard
**Time:** Expect to solve 2 problems in 45 mins

**Tips:**
- Start with brute force, then optimize
- Discuss trade-offs explicitly
- Handle edge cases
- Write clean, production-ready code
- Explain your thinking out loud

### System Design
**Focus:**
- Scalability (millions to billions)
- AWS services knowledge helpful
- Discuss trade-offs extensively
- Cost considerations (frugality LP)

**Common Topics:**
- Design URL shortener
- Design social network feed
- Design e-commerce system
- Design recommendation system

**Tips:**
- Start with requirements
- Do capacity planning
- Think about AWS services (but don't over-rely)
- Discuss monitoring and operations
- Consider cost optimization

### Behavioral (Most Important!)
**Format:** 2 full rounds dedicated to LPs (+ LPs in all rounds)

**Each Question Format:**
- "Tell me about a time when..."
- Follow-up deep dives
- Expect 8-10 LP questions total

**Top LPs Asked:**
1. Customer Obsession
2. Ownership
3. Dive Deep
4. Deliver Results
5. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
6. Bias for Action
7. Learn and Be Curious
8. Earn Trust

**Tips:**
- Prepare 2-3 stories per LP (total 20-25 stories)
- Use STAR method religiously
- Be specific with metrics
- Show ownership even in failures
- Don't speak negatively about others
- Show how you raised the bar
- Demonstrate long-term thinking

### Bar Raiser Round
**What is it:**
- Senior Amazonian from different org
- Has veto power
- Ensures you raise the bar

**How to Handle:**
- This is your toughest round
- Expect hardest technical or most probing behavioral questions
- Stay calm, think out loud
- Show humility and learning mindset

### Level Expectations (14 YOE)

**L6 (Senior SDE):**
- Design and deliver complex systems
- Mentor team members
- Drive technical direction
- Influence without authority

**L7 (Principal SDE):**
- Org-level impact
- Technical strategy
- Multiple team leadership
- Industry expertise

### Preparation Focus
- **40%**: Behavioral (LPs)
- **35%**: Coding
- **25%**: System Design

### Amazon-Specific Resources
- **Book**: "The Amazon Way" by John Rossman
- **Website**: Amazon Leadership Principles page
- **Practice**: Mock behavioral interviews critical

### Offer & Compensation
- **Base**: $170K-220K (L6), $230K-280K (L7)
- **Stock**: Vests over 4 years (5% Y1, 15% Y2, 40% Y3, 40% Y4)
- **Sign-on**: $50K-150K to offset stock vesting
- **Total**: $350K-$500K+ depending on level

**Negotiation Tips:**
- Stock vesting schedule is non-negotiable
- Negotiate sign-on bonus
- Negotiate base (capped at certain levels)
- Consider relocation package

## 2. Google

### Company Culture
- **Innovation**: Solve big problems
- **Data-Driven**: Everything backed by data
- **Collaboration**: Work across teams
- **Technical Excellence**: High technical bar
- **Googleyness**: Humble, collaborative, comfortable with ambiguity

### Interview Process

**Timeline**: 6-8 weeks (including team matching)

**Rounds:**
1. **Phone Screens** (2 rounds, 45 mins each)
   - Pure coding
   - Google Doc coding

2. **Onsite/Virtual** (4-5 hours)
   - **Loop 1-2**: Coding (2 rounds)
   - **Loop 3**: System Design
   - **Loop 4**: Googleyness & Leadership
   - **Optional Loop 5**: Additional coding

3. **Team Matching** (After clearing interviews)
   - Meet with teams
   - Find mutual fit

### Coding Interview
**Focus Areas:**
- Algorithms and Data Structures (heavy focus)
- Graph algorithms
- Dynamic Programming
- Recursion and Backtracking
- String manipulation

**Common Questions:**
- Google search autocomplete
- Find median in data stream
- Word search problems
- Graph traversal problems
- DP problems (LIS, LCS, etc.)

**Difficulty:** Medium to Hard (algorithm-heavy)

**Tips:**
- **Optimal solution expected**: Brute force not enough
- **Code Quality matters**: Clean, modular code
- **Time/Space Complexity**: Always analyze
- **Edge Cases**: Test thoroughly
- **Communication**: Explain approach before coding
- **Google Doc**: Practice coding without IDE

### System Design
**Focus:**
- Handle massive scale (billions of users)
- Google infrastructure knowledge (not required but helpful)
- Clean architecture
- Performance optimization

**Common Topics:**
- Design Google Search
- Design YouTube
- Design Google Drive
- Design Gmail
- Design distributed cache

**Tips:**
- Think about Google-scale problems
- Discuss sharding, partitioning deeply
- Consider data consistency
- Think about latency across globe
- Mention monitoring and alerting

### Googleyness & Leadership
**What They Look For:**
- Collaboration stories
- Handling ambiguity
- Helping others succeed
- Humility despite accomplishments
- Learning from failures

**Sample Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you helped a teammate
- How do you handle ambiguous requirements?
- Describe a time you failed and what you learned
- How do you approach problems you don't know?

**Tips:**
- Show collaborative spirit
- Demonstrate learning mindset
- Be humble, not arrogant
- Show you can work in ambiguity
- Emphasize team success over individual glory

### Level Expectations (14 YOE)

**L5 (Senior Software Engineer):**
- Lead medium-large projects
- Design system components
- Mentor others
- Impact at team level

**L6 (Staff Software Engineer):**
- Lead large, ambiguous projects
- Design full systems
- Influence org-wide decisions
- Impact across multiple teams

### Preparation Focus
- **50%**: Coding (highest emphasis)
- **30%**: System Design
- **20%**: Behavioral/Googleyness

### Google-Specific Resources
- **Practice**: Focus on algorithms
- **Book**: "Algorithms" by Sedgewick
- **Platform**: LeetCode (Google tagged questions)

### Offer & Compensation
- **Base**: $200K-250K (L5), $250K-310K (L6)
- **Stock**: Vests evenly over 4 years (25% per year)
- **Bonus**: 15% of base
- **Total**: $400K-$600K+ depending on level

**Negotiation Tips:**
- Stock refreshers every year (big benefit)
- Negotiate initial grant
- Base has bands
- Sign-on negotiable
- Consider benefits (food, shuttle, etc.)

## General Tips Across All Companies

### Before Applying
1. **Research the company**: Products, culture, recent news
2. **Network**: Connect with employees on LinkedIn
3. **Get referrals**: Significantly increases chances
4. **Target teams**: Know which teams you're interested in
5. **Timing**: Apply when ready, not too early

### During Interview
1. **Communicate clearly**: Think out loud
2. **Ask clarifying questions**: Don't assume
3. **Show enthusiasm**: Genuine interest matters
4. **Be honest**: Don't fake knowledge
5. **Take hints**: Interviewers often guide you
6. **Manage time**: Keep track, don't run over
7. **Stay calm**: It's okay to pause and think

### After Interview
1. **Send thank you**: Email within 24 hours
2. **Reflect**: Note what went well, what didn't
3. **Follow up**: With recruiter after 1 week
4. **Keep practicing**: For other interviews
5. **Don't over-analyze**: Wait for feedback

### Managing Multiple Offers
1. **Timing**: Try to schedule finales close together
2. **Leverage**: Use offers to negotiate others
3. **Compare fairly**: Total comp, not just base
4. **Consider non-monetary**: Culture, team, growth
5. **Ask for time**: Most give 1-2 weeks to decide
